PROJECT 03 / ROBOT MANIPULATION

Robotic Tetris: Vision-guided Manipulation

A ROS-based perception-to-action pipeline for recognising randomly scattered pieces, estimating grasp poses and executing pick, reorientation and placement with a six-axis xArm.

Personal role
Perception and calibration for manipulation
Platform
xArm, RealSense and ROS
Project type
Three-person team project
TASK AND SYSTEM

Close the loop from pixels to robot action.

The team system combined a vision module, a placement-planning module and an xArm motion-control client. A RealSense camera observed a 30 × 30 cm random placement area; the perception output defined each grasp centre and end-effector rotation; ROS services connected those poses to board-placement targets and robot execution.

MY CONTRIBUTION

Perception and calibration for reliable manipulation.

  1. Configured the RealSense acquisition environment and captured the full workspace from a fixed observation pose.
  2. Applied denoising, K-means colour quantisation with k=2 and binary segmentation to isolate all pieces.
  3. Used contours and minimum-area rectangles to extract individual pieces, their centres and orientations.
  4. Classified five piece families with a 2 × 3 occupancy code and handled the bar and square pieces through aspect-ratio rules.
  5. Derived grasp centres and rotation angles, then used nine-point eye-in-hand calibration and least squares to map image coordinates into the robot frame.
ROBOT MOTION AND END EFFECTOR

A deliberately constrained pick-and-place sequence.

  1. Moved the arm to a fixed elevated observation pose and captured all pieces in a single frame to avoid repeated camera motions.
  2. Passed pickup coordinates, placement coordinates and the required wrist angle through a custom ROS service interface.
  3. Rotated the end effector to normalise each piece before placement on the board.
  4. Used point-to-point constant-speed linear motion with vertical approach and retreat segments to reduce collisions and accidental piece displacement.
  5. Controlled the pneumatic suction cup through the controller's digital output and solenoid valve for pick and release.
TEAM PLANNING CONTEXT

Convert manipulation targets into a valid board sequence.

The placement module represented the board as a 14 × 10 occupancy grid and the pieces as 4 × 4 arrays. The report describes depth-first search, binary state compression and dynamic programming or memoisation to reduce repeated placement states. The resulting piece type, board target and wrist rotation were supplied to the motion client in execution order.

LIMITATIONS AND REFLECTION

The report records increasing placement error across repeated trials. By the fourth run, table motion induced by the arm had accumulated enough calibration drift to prevent a complete pick-and-place sequence. A stronger manipulation setup would rigidly isolate the camera, board and robot base, add fiducial-based online correction, and re-estimate the transform when drift exceeds a threshold.
