PROJECT 01 / UNDERGRADUATE THESIS

Physics-consistent Underwater Dehazing

A physics-consistent approach to restoring colour, contrast and detail in turbid underwater images.

Role
Individual undergraduate thesis
Method
Multi-task U-Net + differentiable imaging model
Evaluation
Synthetic, controlled-tank and UIEB images
View source on GitHub ↗
TASK AND CONSTRAINTS

Restoration with limited paired real data.

Underwater light attenuation and backscatter produce colour shifts, veiling haze and loss of local detail. Because aligned clear, turbid and depth observations are difficult to capture in real water, the project needed a controllable way to generate training pairs without discarding the underlying image-formation process.

METHOD
  1. Rendered 217 clear RGB scenes and aligned depth maps in Unreal Engine.
  2. Applied 40 combinations of scattering, illumination and wavelength-dependent attenuation to each scene, producing 8,680 paired samples.
  3. Designed a gated encoder–decoder that jointly predicts a restored image, scene depth and a scattering coefficient.
  4. Re-applied the differentiable degradation model to the predictions and compared the reconstructed turbid image with the original input.
IMPLEMENTATION

One pipeline from physical synthesis to evaluation.

The thesis work covered the single-scattering data generator, scene-level train/validation split, multi-task network, reconstruction losses, training and fine-tuning scripts, controlled-tank acquisition, inference and quantitative evaluation. Scenes—not individual degraded frames—were split between training and validation to reduce scene leakage.

RESULTS AND EVIDENCE
8,680paired synthetic samples
19.56 → 28.24UCIQE on the controlled-tank set
1.25 → 1.74UIQM on the controlled-tank set

The controlled-tank set contained 120 stereo images across 12 turbidity levels and five illumination settings. On UIEB, UCIQE rose from 21.31 to 22.38 while UIQM changed from 2.59 to 2.53, showing that cross-domain colour improvement did not consistently translate into better sharpness and contrast.

LIMITATIONS

The domain gap remains visible.

Training relied mainly on synthetic and controlled data. The single-scattering approximation is less representative at greater depth or in heterogeneous water, and the weaker UIEB result indicates that broader real-water data or domain adaptation would be needed for robust deployment.
